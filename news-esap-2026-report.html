Over 400 participants at ESAP 2026 on the future of animal science education

34th ESAP Annual Conference · Addis Ababa, Ethiopia · 29–30 October 2026

← Back to News

Conference · Ethiopia · 29–30 October 2026

A panel discussion on the conference theme, “Advancing Animal Science Education for Ethiopia’s Livestock Future”. Photo: Yamrot Getahun, Debre Berhan University.

The 34th Annual Conference of the Ethiopian Society of Animal Production (ESAP) took place at the Addis Convention Center in Addis Ababa on 29 and 30 October 2026. Jointly organised by ESAP and ASAP-Bio, it drew more than 400 participants from universities and research institutes, policy and government, and the private sector.

The theme was “Advancing Animal Science Education for Ethiopia’s Livestock Future: Bridging Modern Science and Ethiopian Livestock Production Systems for Sustainable Development”. It put the education of the next generation of animal scientists at the centre of the national conversation on livestock. The conference was also held in the International Year of Rangelands and Pastoralists 2026.

Education alongside the science

The programme combined panel discussions with presentations across eight sub-themes. Curriculum innovation and the modernisation of animal science education came first, followed by genetics and breeding, precision livestock farming, nutrition, feeds and rangeland management, climate resilience, digital agriculture, One Health and food safety, and value chains and policy. Educators, researchers, policy makers and the private sector discussed in the same hall whether graduates are being trained for the livestock sector Ethiopia is building.

ASAP-Bio on the programme

Colleagues from all five ASAP-Bio partner universities took part, coming straight from the partnership’s pedagogy workshop earlier the same week. Teams from Aarhus University, Debre Berhan University and Jomo Kenyatta University of Agriculture and Technology contributed talks.

Grum Gebreyesus (Aarhus University) presented the ASAP-Bio Knowledge Hub, the partnership’s open-access teaching platform. It is free, needs no login, is built for low-bandwidth classrooms, and offers every lecture as a printable PDF. Its first release holds 17 lecture chapters in quantitative genetics and molecular genomics, with worked examples, exercises with solutions and recorded lectures.

Morten Hansen (Aarhus University) showed how Aarhus University teaches animal breeding and reproduction as one integrated, student-centred course, in which student groups work through realistic cases on reproductive and breeding technologies across species and production systems. Colleagues from Debre Berhan University and JKUAT presented their work in the conference sessions.

What comes next

We thank ESAP for the partnership, and everyone who presented and took part. The conversation now moves to Uganda, where the 2nd APSU Scientific Conference, co-organised with ASAP-Bio, takes place on 6 and 7 November with a stream on animal science education.

ASAP-Bio is an African–Danish university partnership in integrated sustainable animal production systems, funded by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Denmark and managed by Danida Fellowship Centre.
